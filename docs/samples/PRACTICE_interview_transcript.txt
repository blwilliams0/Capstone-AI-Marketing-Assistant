PRACTICE TRANSCRIPT - FICTIONAL. Not a real interview. "Sam" is a made-up manager, for testing the app only.

Interviewer: Thanks for doing this, Sam. First question: how would you describe The Pocket to someone who has never been?
Sam: I'd say it's a small, warm place where the food changes with the season and somebody actually remembers you. It's tucked away, so people feel like they found something. I want them to leave thinking, I want to bring my partner here next time.

Interviewer: What makes it different from other upscale restaurants around Carmel and Monterey?
Sam: A lot of places here are beautiful but a bit formal, a bit stiff. We're relaxed without being casual. The covered patio is a big part of it, you can sit outside in January with the heaters on. And the kitchen is small, so the menu stays focused. The first thing a guest notices is probably the patio, honestly, and then the bread.

Interviewer: If The Pocket were a person, how would you describe their personality?
Sam: Someone who's been cooking for twenty years but still gets excited about the first chanterelles of the season. Generous, a little understated, never showing off. Three words I'd use: warm, curious, grounded. Three words I'd never want: trendy, flashy, exclusive.

Interviewer: Okay. When The Pocket talks publicly, on Instagram or emails, how should it sound?
Sam: Conversational. Like a host talking to a regular, not a brand talking to customers. Short sentences. A little bit of humor is fine but it should never feel like we're trying too hard.

Interviewer: Should The Pocket be seen as fun?
Sam: Fun, yes, but grown-up fun. A good bottle shared on the patio is fun. A meme with a dancing chef is not us. I'd rather people say it's lovely than it's a party.

Interviewer: Are there words or styles that should never appear?
Sam: Never say luxury, never say exclusive, never say best in Carmel. No discount language like deal or cheap. Go easy on emojis, maybe one, never a row of them.

Interviewer: Let's talk about guests. Who are your regulars?
Sam: Locals from Carmel and Pacific Grove, a lot of them come once or twice a month. They come back for the staff and because the menu changes. If we told them earlier what's new, I think they'd come more often.

Interviewer: And visitors?
Sam: Mostly people staying at the Inn and a few other hotels nearby, and people who find us on Google Maps. Concierges send us people too, which we don't thank them for enough.

Interviewer: Anything you'd say to locals but not to visitors?
Sam: For locals it's what's new this week and come say hi. For visitors it's this is a real Carmel experience and here's how to book. Same voice, different reason.

Interviewer: What promise does The Pocket make to every guest?
Sam: You'll be looked after like you matter, and the food will be honest. A broken promise would be someone waiting twenty minutes and nobody checking on them.

Interviewer: How should the patio be described?
Sam: Covered and heated, so it works most of the year. Please don't call it outdoor seating only, and don't promise a view, there isn't really an ocean view from there.

Interviewer: How does the menu change, and who confirms dishes?
Sam: Small changes every week, bigger ones each season. Chef confirms any dish before it goes in a post, every time, because things sell out or change at the last minute.

Interviewer: How important is wine?
Sam: Very. We have a short list with a lot of local Monterey and Santa Lucia Highlands producers. Pairings are something we'd like to talk about more.

Interviewer: What does personal hospitality mean here?
Sam: Remembering names and what people like. Little things, like bringing the decaf without being asked.

Interviewer: How do you feel about promotions and discounts?
Sam: No discounts. A wine dinner with a set price is fine, that's an event, not a sale. Anything that feels like a coupon cheapens us.

Interviewer: What should marketing never say or show?
Sam: Never show guests' faces without asking them. Never compare us to other restaurants by name. No prices in posts unless it's an event.

Interviewer: Who has to approve things before they go out?
Sam: Anything about food, Chef has to see it. Anything about events or prices, the GM. Day-to-day posts, either one of us is fine.

Interviewer: Last one. Six months from now, what would tell you marketing is working?
Sam: More locals coming back during the week, especially Tuesday to Thursday, and the patio full on cold nights. And honestly, me spending less time thinking about Instagram.

Interviewer: Great, thank you, Sam.
Sam: Thanks, this was fun.
