# Part 1 — Strategic Approach

The Pocket should use a focused-differentiation marketing strategy built around one brand and two priority audiences: locals and tourists.

The restaurant should communicate the same core identity to both groups: an intimate, upscale Carmel dining experience built around seasonal food, personal hospitality, wine, and its covered patio, but adjust the reason for visiting based on the audience.

This strategy builds on what The Pocket already does well rather than asking the business to create an entirely new identity or rely primarily on discounts.

## Strategic Problem

The Pocket has strong reasons for customers to visit, but those strengths are not always communicated consistently across digital platforms, referrals, customer relationships, and event opportunities.

The marketing challenge is therefore not creating a better dining experience. It is helping more appropriate customers discover, understand, and return to the experience The Pocket already provides.

## Marketing Objectives

The strategy should help The Pocket:

- Increase awareness among relevant visitors and local customers.
- Give each priority audience a clear reason to choose The Pocket.
- Encourage repeat visits by communicating seasonal changes and special experiences.
- Strengthen referrals from the Inn, hotels, concierges, and local relationships.
- Support qualified private-event interest.
- Create a manageable way to organize marketing and understand what is producing meaningful results.

## Priority Audiences

| Audience | Locals and repeat customers | Tourists and visitors |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketing purpose** | Retention and relationship building | Discovery and first-time consideration |
| **Message emphasis** | Seasonal menu changes, personal recognition, wine experiences, hospitality, and reasons to return | Distinctive Carmel experience, patio, chef-driven food, atmosphere, service, and convenient booking |
| **Primary outcomes** | Repeat reservations, event participation, local word of mouth | First-time reservations, Inn referrals, hotel and concierge traffic |

Private events should support both audiences rather than operate as a separate primary segment. Local customers may attend or organize events, while visitors may discover The Pocket through group dining or special occasions.

## Positioning

> "The Pocket is an intimate, upscale Carmel dining experience where seasonal culinary discovery, personal hospitality, wine, and a distinctive covered patio come together."

The positioning should communicate the combination of these qualities. The patio, chef, menu, wine program, and service should function as evidence of the larger experience rather than as disconnected promotional messages.

## Strategic Recommendations

### 1. Build a management-approved brand foundation

Management should define and approve:

- The Pocket's most important differentiators
- The tone and personality of its communication
- The types of messages that fit or do not fit the brand
- The approved descriptions of the restaurant, menu, patio, events, and hospitality
- The information staff can use when communicating with customers and partners

This ensures that marketing reflects the knowledge of the people who understand the restaurant best.

### 2. Use different messages for locals and tourists

For locals, marketing should focus on reasons to return:

- Frequent menu changes
- Seasonal ingredients and dishes
- Wine dinners and special experiences
- New desserts or chef features
- Personal hospitality and recognition
- Local relationships and word of mouth

For tourists, marketing should focus on reasons to choose The Pocket:

- A memorable Carmel dining experience
- The covered and heated patio
- Seasonal, chef-driven food
- Wine and atmosphere
- Positive guest experience
- Clear information and convenient reservations

The strategy should tailor the message without creating two separate brands.

### 3. Assign each channel a clear role

| Channel | Strategic role |
|---|---|
| Google Business Profile and search | Help tourists and other active searchers discover The Pocket |
| Website and reservation platforms | Explain the experience accurately and make booking easy |
| Email or opt-in customer communication | Give locals and repeat customers reasons to return |
| Social media | Show the people, food, patio, seasonal changes, and atmosphere |
| Inn, hotel, and concierge relationships | Reach visitors who are already looking for local dining |
| Local partnerships and referrals | Support awareness and trusted word of mouth |
| Private-event information | Make the restaurant's event capabilities easier to understand and inquire about |

The strategy does not require equal attention to every channel. Management can emphasize the channels that best fit current priorities and capacity.

### 4. Promote existing experiences

The Pocket should use what is already happening in the restaurant as ongoing marketing material:

- Seasonal menu changes
- Chef and pastry expertise
- Patio dining
- Wine pairings and dinners
- Hospitality and staff knowledge
- Private dining and special events
- The connection between the Inn and restaurant

This creates a sustainable source of communication without requiring constant discounts or artificial promotions.

### 5. Create simple ownership and support

Management should retain final authority over the brand, positioning, budget, and major partnerships.

A designated marketing lead should coordinate approved marketing activity, collect current menu and event information, maintain basic records, and communicate with the relevant staff.

Chef and kitchen leadership should verify food-related information. Front-of-house and reservations staff can contribute guest insights and referral feedback.

The proposed AI marketing assistant can support the process by:

- Storing approved brand and restaurant information
- Organizing marketing ideas
- Drafting content options for management review
- Adapting messages for locals and tourists
- Suggesting appropriate channels
- Organizing basic performance information

The AI tool should remain advisory. It should not independently publish content, change the brand, or make decisions about spending and customer relationships.

### 6. Measure useful outcomes

The Pocket should use low-effort measures connected to the strategy:

- **Tourist discovery:** first-time reservations, Inn and hotel referrals, reservation-link activity
- **Local retention:** repeat reservations, opt-in communication response, event participation
- **Event opportunity:** qualified inquiries, bookings, and event revenue when available
- **Marketing efficiency:** staff time, direct costs, and confirmed bookings where measurable
- **Brand quality:** management review, guest feedback, and factual accuracy

Views and clicks can be useful signals, but they should not be treated as proof of business results by themselves.

## Strategic Flexibility

The core strategy should remain consistent, while the specific marketing activities can change.

If visitor demand is the priority, The Pocket can emphasize Google, the website, Inn referrals, hotels, and concierges. If local retention is the priority, it can emphasize seasonal updates, customer relationships, email, and local events. If private-event demand becomes more important, it can emphasize event information and referral relationships.

This makes the strategy specific without making it rigid. The direction stays the same, but management can apply it according to capacity, seasonality, staffing, and business priorities.

## Final Strategic Statement

The Pocket should use one consistent brand to serve two priority audiences: locals who need reasons to return and tourists who need reasons to discover the restaurant. By communicating its existing strengths through audience-appropriate channels, strengthening referral relationships, and using simple internal ownership and measurement, The Pocket can improve marketing effectiveness without compromising its personal, upscale identity.

# Part 2 — Actionable Strategy

### 1. Define the brand foundation

Management identifies and approves:

- The Pocket's key differentiators
- Preferred tone and personality
- Main customer promises
- Messages or promotions that do not fit the brand

**AI assistant role:** Organize management's answers into an approved brand guide and reference library.

### 2. Build two audience paths

Create simple messaging for:

- **Locals:** seasonal menu changes, personal hospitality, wine events, and reasons to return
- **Tourists:** Carmel experience, patio, chef-driven food, atmosphere, and convenient reservations

**AI assistant role:** Adapt approved brand messages for each audience without changing the core identity.

### 3. Strengthen the main discovery channels

Keep information accurate and consistent across:

- Google Business Profile
- Website and reservation platforms
- Social media
- Inn, hotel, and concierge materials
- Event and referral information

**AI assistant role:** Identify outdated information, suggest updates, and draft approved descriptions.

### 4. Promote what already exists

Regularly communicate:

- Seasonal dishes and menu changes
- Patio dining
- Chef and pastry expertise
- Wine experiences
- Hospitality and staff knowledge
- Private events

**AI assistant role:** Turn current menu and event information into content ideas and drafts for management review.

### 5. Assign ownership

| Owner | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Management | Owns the brand, budget, approvals, and major partnerships |
| Marketing lead | Coordinates information, channels, and basic tracking |
| Chef and staff | Verify food and guest-experience details |
| AI assistant | Organizes, drafts, suggests, and summarizes |
| Outside specialists | Support technical work when needed |

### 6. Track a few useful results

Review:

- New and repeat reservations
- Referral sources
- Inn and hotel referrals
- Private-event inquiries
- Customer engagement
- Staff time and marketing workload

Management can then continue, adjust, or pause activities based on what appears useful.

## Overall Principle

The Pocket can apply this plan selectively. The strategy stays consistent, but the specific activities can change depending on seasonality, staffing, capacity, and business priorities.
