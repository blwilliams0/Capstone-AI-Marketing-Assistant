## 1. AI-drafted proposal based on Nick's work

### The Pocket — Proposed Marketing Strategy
*Working proposal for team review | Management owns the brand*

The Pocket needs an organized marketing strategy before choosing any particular software. The business already delivers a distinctive experience, but its marketing lacks consistent ownership, a repeatable process, and a reliable way to connect outreach to reservations and revenue. The strategy below is a research-informed proposal, not a claim that its financial impact has already been proven.

#### 1. Brand direction and objectives

Management and ownership decide what The Pocket stands for, which customers it wants to attract, the tone of the brand, and what kinds of advertising are inappropriate. Structured brand interviews should capture these decisions before campaigns begin. The proposed positioning is an intimate, upscale Carmel restaurant where seasonal discovery, personal hospitality, wine, and the covered and heated courtyard come together.

Objectives: increase awareness among appropriate visitors, strengthen repeat visits among locals, improve qualified private-event inquiries, and make marketing manageable and measurable without replacing the personal hospitality central to the restaurant.

#### 2. Dual-segment approach

| Decision | Locals and repeat guests | Tourists and visitors |
|---|---|---|
| Reason to choose | Something new with the familiarity of a trusted restaurant | A distinctive Carmel dining experience for the visit |
| What to emphasize | Seasonal menu changes, regular-customer hospitality, wine dinners, new desserts | Atmosphere, patio, culinary story, service, convenient booking |
| Priority channels | Opt-in email, local referrals, events, appropriate social posts | Google Search/Maps, website and SEO, hotels/concierges, tourism partners |
| Primary outcomes | Repeat reservations, event participation | First-time reservations, qualified referral traffic |

Private-event clients are a campaign type across both segments, rather than a substitute for the two-segment strategy.

#### 3. Marketing programs

**Brand story and content.** Build a management-approved library of accurate restaurant stories: why the menu changes, chef and pastry capabilities, the patio, wine experiences, the people behind service, and private events. Use quality photography and authentic explanations instead of generic promotions. Verify current dishes and availability each time because the menu changes frequently.

**Discovery and referrals.** Improve Google Business Profile, website descriptions, search relevance, photos, reservation links, and consistent listings. Give hotels, Inn The Pocket, concierges, and tourism partners approved descriptions and clear referral information. Evaluate appropriate local print or community channels rather than assuming digital is the only option.

**Local retention and special occasions.** Offer opt-in seasonal updates, event invitations, and occasional restaurant news. Give locals new reasons to return without relying primarily on discounts. Promote wine dinners, private dining, and dessert experiences only when management confirms capacity, pricing, and operational feasibility.

#### 4. Clear responsibilities: who owns what

| Owner | Responsibility | Decision authority |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership and senior management | Approve positioning, audiences, brand boundaries, marketing budget, success criteria, and major partnerships | Final authority over brand and spend |
| Designated marketing lead (existing staff or part-time role) | Maintain calendar, collect current menu/event facts and photos, coordinate campaigns, collect metrics, request approvals | Operational owner; no unilateral brand changes |
| Chef / kitchen leadership | Verify dish names, ingredients, availability, culinary stories, photography accuracy | Approve food-specific factual claims |
| Front-of-house / reservations team | Provide approved guest insights, referral feedback, and reservation trends without intrusive questioning | Use approved privacy practices |
| AI marketing assistant, if adopted | Guide staff, draft options, maintain approved brand knowledge, suggest channels and photo guidance, organize tests, summarize results | Advisory only; no autonomous posting or brand changes |
| Outside specialists, if used | Targeted photography, SEO technical work, or platform setup | Execute agreed scope; management retains control |

If no employee can realistically own marketing, management should assess hiring a part-time employee or a limited-scope external provider. AI does not remove the need for a human owner.

#### 5. Process and approvals

A weekly workflow should be simple: identify the marketing priority, verify current restaurant information, choose the audience and channel, draft and approve the message, publish through an authorized employee, then review a few meaningful results. Management signs off on brand-sensitive campaigns and material changes; food facts are checked with kitchen leadership. The AI assistant may help at each step but never publishes automatically.

#### 6. Measures, tests, and budget

| Goal | Indicator | Limit / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Tourist acquisition | First-time reservations, hotel/Inn referrals, reservation-link conversions | Website clicks alone are not diners |
| Local retention | Repeat reservations, opt-in email response, event attendance | Separate genuine repeat behavior from impressions |
| Event demand | Qualified inquiries, bookings, event revenue where available | Check whether event demand displaces ordinary dining |
| Marketing efficiency | Hours spent, direct expenses, cost per confirmed booking where measurable | Track staff time; compare with alternatives |
| Brand quality | Management review, guest feedback, factual accuracy | Brand consistency is not reducible to clicks |

Start with existing operational data and low-effort tracking. Avoid asking every guest intrusive questions. Use consent-based customer information and keep estimates separate from verified bookings and sales.

#### 7. Phased rollout and alternatives

Phase 1: management brand interviews, ownership assignment, baseline discovery audit and available reservation measures. Phase 2: test one local-focused and one visitor-focused campaign; correct Google and website basics. Phase 3: compare outcomes, workload and cost; expand successful practices. Compare status quo, a documented template system, a part-time marketer, an agency, and an AI-assisted workflow before deciding on implementation.

#### Evidence and assumptions

Client interview and prior internal analysis support the marketing ownership, time, and attribution gaps; the proposed positioning builds on existing restaurant capabilities. Industry research suggests that discovery, experiential marketing, and repeat-guest communication matter, but does not establish a specific return for The Pocket. Market validation, management approval, and baseline performance figures remain necessary.

Source context: The Pocket client interview; The Pocket Marketing Strategy (team working document); team market/competitor and internal analysis; Google Business Profile restaurant guidance; SevenRooms 2025 diner survey; OpenTable dining-trend materials. ## 2. Nick's original work

> **Authorship:** Nick (capstone team member), as recorded by the team.

### Marketing Strategy

The Pocket has the ingredients of a strong marketing position already within the business. A flexible marketing approach could help organize and communicate those strengths more consistently, while allowing management to choose the activities that best fit its capacity and priorities.

#### 1. Strategic premise
The Pocket already offers a distinctive experience built around personal hospitality, seasonal food, wine, chef expertise, and its patio. The opportunity is to communicate those strengths more consistently to the audiences most likely to value them.

#### 2. Core marketing challenge
The Pocket has several compelling reasons to visit, but those reasons are not always communicated consistently across digital platforms, referrals, customer relationships, and event opportunities.

#### 3. Strategic objective
The strategy should help The Pocket:
- Increase awareness among relevant local and visitor audiences
- Give potential customers clearer reasons to choose The Pocket
- Encourage repeat visits by highlighting seasonal change and personal connection
- Strengthen private-event and referral opportunities
- Create a manageable way to organize and evaluate marketing activity

#### 4. Priority audiences

**Local and repeat customers.** The Pocket can appeal to locals by emphasizing familiarity, personal recognition, changing menus, seasonal discovery, and reasons to return.

**Visitors and Inn guests.** The Pocket can appeal to visitors by presenting itself as a memorable Carmel dining experience rather than simply another restaurant option.

**Private-event customers.** The Pocket can appeal to event customers through its ability to create a more personal gathering experience.

#### 5. Positioning direction
The Pocket should be positioned as an intimate, upscale Carmel dining experience where seasonal food, personal hospitality, wine, and atmosphere come together.

The emphasis should be on the combination of these qualities.

#### 6. Strategic recommendations
- Develop a consistent brand story centered on seasonal discovery and personal hospitality.
- Use digital platforms to make The Pocket easier to find and easier to understand.
- Overhaul SEO
- Use the changing menu and patio as recurring reasons for customers to return or visit.
- Strengthen relationships with the Inn, concierges, hotels, tourism partners, and local organizations.
- Present private events as an extension of The Pocket's hospitality rather than as a separate business.
- Use customer information and simple feedback systems to support repeat visits.
- Track enough information to understand what creates interest without creating a burdensome reporting system.
- Use an AI marketing assistant as a support tool for organizing ideas, guiding management on all marketing tasks, maintaining consistency, and reducing management workload.

#### 7. Flexible application
The recommendations should work as a menu of strategic options rather than a fixed sequence of steps.

The Pocket could begin with whichever opportunities best fit its current capacity, season, staffing, and priorities. For example:
- If visitor demand is the immediate priority, emphasize Google visibility, Inn referrals, and tourism partnerships.
- If local repeat business is the priority, emphasize seasonal menu communication, email, and relationship marketing.
- If event demand is the priority, emphasize event messaging, referral relationships, and clearer inquiry materials.

The strategy remains consistent even when the specific tactics change.

#### 8. Measurement direction
The Pocket could evaluate progress through practical indicators such as:
- New and repeat reservations
- Customer referral sources
- Inn and hotel referrals
- Private-event inquiries
- Customer engagement with seasonal content
- Review activity and recurring guest feedback
- Whether management feels the marketing process is manageable and useful

The strategy should avoid making measurement feel like a new administrative burden.

#### 9. Role of the proposed solution
The AI marketing assistant should be presented as a support system for the strategy. It could help The Pocket:
- Store brand and customer knowledge
- Organize marketing ideas
- Suggest content based on current menus and events
- Adapt messaging for locals, visitors, and event customers
- Maintain a flexible marketing calendar
- Help review basic performance information
- Assist on where to reach out (digital, print)

## 3. Presentation script, slides 11 and 12

Slide deck: https://canva.link/gsmwmkknoj5goto

### Slide 11

The Pocket has two main customer groups, those being locals and repeat customers, and tourists and visitors. Both of which value the same dining experience, but they may need different reasons to either choose to come in the first place, or return.

For locals, the focus would be on retention. Seasonal menu changes, wine dinners, special events, and personalized hospitality can give them reasons to come back.

For tourists, the focus is discovery. The Pocket can emphasize its distinctive Carmel experience, including the patio, chef-driven menu, and atmosphere. Visibility could be strengthened through Google, hotel and Inn recommendations, concierge referrals, and other discovery channels.

The key is to tailor the message without creating two separate brands. Both audiences should see the same core identity, that being an upscale, personal, and seasonal dining experience.

Overall, the strategy is meant to support awareness, repeat visits, referrals, and event interest while preserving what already makes The Pocket unique.

Now that we know who The Pocket is trying to reach, how can they communicate with those audiences more consistently?

### Slide 12

Our recommendations focus on four manageable areas.

First, establish a consistent brand identity by clearly defining The Pocket's main differentiators, tone, and message.

Second, improve digital discoverability by making sure Google, reservation platforms, and online listings accurately reflect the experience.

Third, strengthen referral partnerships with the Inn, hotels, concierges, and tourism organizations.

Fourth, promote the experiences The Pocket already offers, such as seasonal dishes, wine dinners, the patio, chef expertise, and special events.

These efforts could be supported by a simple marketing process with clear responsibility and basic tracking.

The goal is not to completely change The Pocket's marketing or create unnecessary work. It is to organize what the business already does well in a way that management can realistically maintain.

So the next question is how to support this strategy without adding more pressure to an already busy management team, and that's where our proposal comes in.

## 4. Summary of how the strategy was made more actionable

We made the marketing strategy more actionable by moving from broad recommendations to clear areas of responsibility. The strategy now identifies two priority audiences—locals and tourists—and explains how the message should differ for each. It also gives each channel a purpose, such as using Google and referrals for visitor discovery and seasonal updates or events for local retention.

We also clarified who is responsible for what. Management remains in control of the brand, staff provide accurate restaurant information, and the AI assistant supports organization, content drafts, and basic tracking. Finally, we included practical measures like new and repeat reservations, referral sources, event inquiries, and staff workload. This makes the strategy easier to apply and evaluate without forcing The Pocket into a rigid campaign schedule.

**Two audiences → channel roles → clear ownership → simple measurement.**
